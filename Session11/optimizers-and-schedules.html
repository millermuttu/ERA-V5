The rules are not the same expression. The lesson writes η·m̂/(√v̂ + ε). PyTorch computes (η/(1−β1t))·m / (√v/√(1−β2t) + ε), putting ε outside the second moment's correction. That form reproduces torch's output to 4.8e-17, so the whole difference is the epsilon term — invisible here, and growing as √v̂ falls toward ε.
Non-determinism outruns the thing being measured. Two separate runs of the same script land 3.14e-05 apart on the same tensor after one step — thirty times the step itself, from reduction order in the backward kernels. The check only means something when both rules are handed one gradient tensor inside one run.

02  Bias correction

Twenty steps is two orders of magnitude too short

The ratio between the corrected and uncorrected step has the gradients cancel out of it: √(1 − β2t) / (1 − β1t) — a property of the betas alone, matching the simulation to 1.9e-07.

3.16×uncorrected step at t = 1
6.24×uncorrected step at t = 20 — it got worse
3,916first step within 1%
6,213first step within 0.1%

After twenty steps the two weights are 0.0991 apart, on a weight that started at 1.0. The difference does not stop mattering inside the window — it grows through it, and the answer to the question lives near step 4,000.

04  Cosine against WSD

Cosine wins the question as asked, and loses the decision

Three seeds each, 300 planned steps, identical data order, held-out loss on a fixed 20-batch slice of val.bin. WSD is the widget's shape: 2% warmup, flat to 90%, linear decay over the last 10%.

held-out loss at step 200 at step 300
cosine2.5081 ± 0.00392.4862 ± 0.0078
WSD2.5158 ± 0.00692.4602 ± 0.0033
WSD checkpoint at 200, decayed over 20 extra steps 2.4871 ± 0.0030—
Which model would I keep? Neither of the two on offer. Stopped cold at 200, cosine leads by 0.0077 against a seed spread of 0.0069 — about one standard deviation, real but small. WSD's step-200 checkpoint decayed over twenty more steps reaches 2.4871: better than cosine-at-200 by three seed-spreads, for 10% more compute, and available at any step of the run rather than only at a total fixed in advance. Cosine stopped early cannot be repaired.

Confidence, in order. High in the muP value: a measurement repeated at three widths spanning 16× in parameter count, landing in the same cell every time, on a grid whose resolution makes the claim “within a factor of 2.6”. Low in the extrapolation: a three-point fit stretched two more octaves, whose slope (−1.37) disagrees with the rule it is meant to instantiate, and which lands 30% away from what the widget's −1 would give from the same width-256 point. Both arms are 4 layers, 400 steps, 65-token vocabulary; depth, run length and vocabulary all move the optimum and none were varied.
