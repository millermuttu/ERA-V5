Why mix memory systems across depth?

A model does not have to make every layer remember in the same way. Change the eight-layer schedule and watch the central tradeoff.

Compare a schedule

One eight-layer motif

The sequence moves upward through every layer. A G layer is an occasional chance to read selected earlier tokens directly.

input→
→deeper
D · fixed-state layerCompresses the past into a fixed-size running state. Cheap to serve, but old token detail is not kept exactly.
G · sparse-attention layerReads a selected set of earlier K/V entries directly. It restores exact token access, but those entries require a growing KV cache.

What the evidence supports: LightningLM V4 used DDDGDDDG—six DeltaNet layers and two sparse-attention layers—and kept that motif from the 1.78B seed to the 120B run. It shows that this mixture worked. Because the neighboring ratios were not cleanly ablated, it does not show that 6D : 2G is optimal.
V4-style arithmetic, 1G → 8G KV state: 8.0× mixing compute: about 1.41×

The comparison schedules are illustrations, not reported V4 ablations. The key idea is the direction of the trade: more G layers provide more frequent direct access; more D layers reduce the per-sequence memory bill.
