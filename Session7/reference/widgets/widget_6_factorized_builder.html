Spectrum decay
How concentrated the table's content is. Every matrix can be written as a sum of independent directions with a magnitude each, largest first. That ordered list of magnitudes is its spectrum, and the decay is how fast the list falls away. At this setting half of the table's content sits in its first directions, out of . High decay means the table is nearly low-rank already, so a narrow bottleneck loses almost nothing and factorization is close to free. Low decay means the table genuinely uses all its directions, so any bottleneck throws away real capacity in proportion. This is not a knob you own. It is a property of the embedding table the model actually learns, and it is the single thing that decides whether factorization works. Retained capacity against rank

The reference table is built as a sum of rank-one components whose magnitudes decay at the rate you set, so retained energy at rank r is the exact ratio of squared magnitudes kept to total. The vertical marker is your chosen rank. Flatten the decay and the same rank keeps less, which is the honest version of "it depends on the data".
