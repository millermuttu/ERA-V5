The Wall at max_position

A learned absolute position table is trained live in your browser on a task whose answer depends only on where a token sits. Training visits positions zero through the boundary you choose and never goes past it. Then the same trained model is evaluated across the full range. The cliff is measured, not drawn.

The position table itself

Each row is one position's learned vector. Rows inside the trained range were gathered by a batch and written to by the scatter-add. Rows past the boundary were never gathered, so nothing ever wrote to them and they still hold the values they were initialised with.

There is no parameter anywhere in this model that connects row to row . They are independent rows in a lookup table, so no amount of extra training inside the range can teach the model anything about what lies past it. That is the wall, and it is the reason the modern schemes compute position from a function of t instead of storing it.
